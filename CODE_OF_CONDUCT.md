# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be straightforward and be kind. Those are not in tension, and where they seem
to be, the problem is usually the phrasing rather than the content.

Concretely, in this project:

- **Criticise the work, not the person.** "This will drop a zone's signing key
  on the second apply" is a good review comment. "You clearly did not think
  about this" is not, and it says less.
- **Bring the evidence.** A claim about PowerDNS behaviour needs a source and a
  round-trip, from anybody, including a maintainer. Disagreement here is
  settled by the lab, not by seniority.
- **Assume the other person read the same documentation and reached a
  different conclusion.** In this codebase that has usually meant the
  documentation was wrong; ten such cases are recorded in `docs/plan.md`.
- **A question is not an imposition.** Somebody asking why the provider works
  a certain way has found a place where it is not obvious. That is useful.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks; discriminatory or demeaning language;
publishing anyone's private information; sustained disruption. This holds in
issues, pull requests, commit messages and anywhere else the project is
discussed.

## Enforcement

Report to <admin@lavrukhin.net>. Reports are handled privately, and the person
reporting is not required to have proof that would satisfy a court.

Maintainers may remove content, close a thread, or block an account. The
response is proportionate to what happened and to whether it is repeated. A
maintainer who breaches this holds themselves to it too — being the person who
merges does not put anyone outside it.

## Scope

This applies to project spaces and to public spaces where somebody is
representing the project.
